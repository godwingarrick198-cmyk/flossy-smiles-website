Dental Implants · Coral Gables, FL

A Natural-Looking, Long-Term Tooth Replacement

Dental implants can be a very effective long-term solution for missing teeth, closely imitating the look and function of a natural tooth.

What Are Dental Implants?

How Dental Implants Work

Dental implants are small, screw-shaped titanium posts that function similarly to the root of a natural tooth. Once placed in the jawbone, the posts fuse with the bone to create a solid base for a replacement tooth. The presence of the implant also stimulates new bone growth in the jaw.

If a patient has insufficient bone quantity, a bone graft may be necessary before implant placement. Most patients undergo implant placement under local anesthesia.

Evaluation & Planning

What to Expect During Your Consultation

Due to the surgical nature of the process, our team performs a comprehensive evaluation to determine your eligibility for implant treatment. This may include x-rays, teeth models, CT scans, and a precision surgical guide to help determine your bite, the proper positioning of the implant, and the quality and density of your jawbone.

  • Comprehensive oral exam and imaging
  • Discussion of bone grafting, if needed
  • A treatment plan built around the number of teeth being replaced
  • Review of anesthesia and sedation options

Full-Arch Solutions

All-on-4 & All-on-X: Teeth in a Day

Instead of waiting for several months to have replacement teeth attached, many All-on-4 patients can have them attached the same day. This approach uses strategically placed implants to support a permanent, fixed set of replacement teeth.

Best For

Extensive Tooth Loss

Patients with no teeth, or teeth that are failing, are typically good candidates for All-on-4 or All-on-X.

Process

Fewer Implants Needed

Because it doesn't require bone grafts or a large number of implants, treatment often takes less time overall.

Result

Natural-Looking Function

Patients can feel more at ease chewing, speaking, and socializing with a stable, fixed set of teeth.

Implants FAQ

Common Questions About Dental Implants

What are dental implants made of?

Dental implants are small, screw-shaped titanium posts placed in the jawbone to act as an artificial tooth root, supporting a replacement crown.

Will I need a bone graft?

Not every patient does. If a patient has insufficient bone quantity in the jaw, a bone graft may be recommended before implant placement to build a stable foundation.

Is implant surgery painful?

Most patients undergo implant placement under local anesthesia. Sedation dentistry options are also available for patients who experience anxiety or sensitivity.

How is my treatment planned?

Our team performs a comprehensive evaluation using x-rays, teeth models, CT scans, and a precision surgical guide to determine your bite, implant positioning, and jawbone quality before recommending a plan.

What is the difference between a single implant and All-on-4?

A single implant replaces one missing tooth. All-on-4 and All-on-X use several strategically placed implants to support a full, fixed arch of replacement teeth for patients with extensive tooth loss.
